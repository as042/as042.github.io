Ever since I was a small child, I wanted to know how every little thing in the universe worked. From the software on my computer, to my own human biology, I wanted to understand it all, and I wanted evidence. In that way, I guess I was kind of born to be a scientist.

I am currently a 4th-year Schreyer Honors student and undergraduate research assistant at Penn State. In my courses, I study biology at both the molecular and microscopic levels, and in Nekrutenko's lab, I develop the computational tools and workflows with which scientists research these topics. I get the best of both worlds!

Research Interests

I am committed to the development of open-source, accessible science, and in the Nekrutenko Lab, I pursue this goal via the following:

  • SRA metadata enrichment: the Sequence Read Archive is far too large to clean by hand but not too large for LLMs.
  • Planetary genomic surveillance: why analyze one sequence at a time when we can monitor almost the entire planet?
  • Pathogen selection analysis: using novel, model-based software, we are studying rapidly-diverging new pathogens.

Projects & Software

All repositories: github.com/as042?tab=repositories

Disassembler

Streams Logan cDBG unitigs from S3, anchors a query coding sequence, and walks the local subgraph to reconstruct intra-host coding haplotypes.

phylogeny-project

A lightweight Rust library that builds phylogenetic trees from distance matrices with neighbor joining and renders them to Newick, SVG, or PNG.

turing-machine-sim

A dependency-free Rust Turing machine simulator, with a brute-force experiment that approximates Chaitin's halting probability.
